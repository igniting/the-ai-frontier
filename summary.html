One Claude Code session, checked end to end

Two languages, a theorem, and a check for every claim

In one long session, Claude built two programming languages from scratch, with their compilers, a SAT solver, and a machine-checked proof that the Schur number S(4) is 44. Each layer was checked by the one below it, and every claim here can be re-run from the repository with Node 22 and nothing else.

What was built

Lumen

A small typed language, compiled to WebAssembly with a garbage collector.

  • A compiler, and a reference interpreter to compare it with.
  • A second compiler, written in Lumen, that compiles itself to itself.
  • 40 rewrite rules found by search, each proved with SAT.
  • An optimizer tuned in eight measured, gated steps.

A theorem

S(4) = 44: the numbers 1 to 44 split into four groups with no x, y and x + y in one group, and 1 to 45 cannot.

  • A SAT solver written in Lumen refutes 1 to 45 in 255,663 steps.
  • Lumen 2 prints the same refutation, byte for byte, and on four cores proves the theorem in 22 seconds.

Lumen 2

A second language, designed from what the first one taught.

  • Types say what a function can do: print, read, use the clock or randomness, or fail.
  • Array accesses proved safe, each proof a SAT refutation with a certificate.
  • Memory without a garbage collector, by reference counting.
  • A compiler of about 8,600 lines of Lumen 2 that compiles itself, after a port that matched the JavaScript original on 1,086 programs.
  • Users' functions over files, on every core, with step budgets and incremental re-runs.

What it shows, and what it doesn't

It shows

  • Sustained engineering over one long session, in which each layer was checked by the one below it, including the mistakes those checks caught, which the story lists.
  • That every claim can be re-run: the commands are in the repository, and CI runs all but the timings on every push.

It doesn't show

  • That earlier models could not have done it. CHALLENGE.md turns the task into a benchmark with a scorer, for anyone who wants to compare.
  • A new result in mathematics: S(4) = 44 has been known since 1965. What is new is that a language built in the session produced the proof.
  • That Lumen 2 is worth adopting. It is slower than C, and its wins come from what its checker proves. In a simulated month of a daily pipeline, its incremental re-runs were right every day, but the computing they saved was worth about a cent.
  • Anything beyond one machine: every timing comes from one 4-core machine and one JavaScript engine.
